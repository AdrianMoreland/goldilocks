## Purpose
The rules in the customer-facing General Terms and Conditions (v1.02, 2023) that staff are asked about most. The full terms are the legal document. If a customer disputes anything, refer to a manager.

## Orders and prices
1. Any estimate is an Initial Quote. It is indicative and reflects live spot at that moment ([[customer-inquiry-to-quote#quote-validity]]).
2. The final price is confirmed when we receive funds, or cash at the premises ([[payment-lock-and-hedge]]).
3. If the price in the pricing email contains an error, tell the customer. They can reconfirm at the correct price or cancel. If we cannot reach them, treat it as cancelled.
4. Orders are subject to verification, receipt of payment and stock availability. If stock is not available the customer may wait or cancel. We send an email confirmation after the order.
5. Customers must be 18 or over.

1. Collect from the vault location on the quote. We do not deliver to private or company addresses.
2. Orders are ready in 7 to 10 working days where cleared funds arrive before 3pm. We are not liable for delays outside our control.
3. Ownership passes on collection once the full value is received.

## Cancellation and returns
1. There is no right of return, and the distance-selling cancellation right does not apply because prices depend on financial markets.
2. Where practicable we make reasonable efforts to help a customer cancel before the order is fulfilled by buying the bullion back. The customer must phone straight away.

## Liability and tax
1. We give a warranty of reasonable skill and care only, and are liable only for foreseeable losses.
2. Investments can fall as well as rise.
3. The customer is solely responsible for their own tax affairs, including any capital gains tax. We give no tax, legal or accounting advice.
4. Irish law and Irish courts apply.
5. Merrion Gold is not regulated by the Central Bank of Ireland. It is regulated by the Department of Justice for AML.

## Privacy
- Personal data is handled under GDPR. A complaint goes to the Data Protection Commission. Data may be used for anti-fraud checks.
