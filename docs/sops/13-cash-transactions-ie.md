## Purpose
Take large cash payments safely, check where the money comes from, and record every such transaction.
Sources: AML Policy (Dec 2022), MG AML Risk Policy (Nov 2023), Cash Transaction Record Form (2026), Selling gold procedure.

## When it applies
- Cash of €10,000 or more in one payment, or in a series of linked payments by the same customer.
- This applies when the customer pays cash for goods and when we pay cash. Buybacks are normally paid by bank transfer only ([[customer-buyback#paying-the-customer]]).
- The threshold is €10,000 or more. A payment of exactly €10,000 is covered.
- Cash payments by the same customer are linked if they add up to €10,000 or more within 12 months.
- Customer email wording must never say that no ID is needed under €10,000. See [[customer-email-wording#cash-purchases]].

## Before accepting the cash
1. Do not accept the cash until all checks below are done.
2. Photo ID and recent proof of address ([[kyc-aml#accepted-photo-id]], [[kyc-aml#accepted-proof-of-address]]).
3. Source of funds (see [[cash-transactions-ie#source-of-funds]]).
4. Screening: PEP, sanctions, adverse media.
5. Check the customer, the payer and the collector are the same person. If not, stop and ask a manager.
6. Count the cash with a second person ([[desk-security-and-dual-control#dual-control]]).
7. Record the note denominations and condition. If notes are banded, record the bank, branch and date on the band, keep the bands and attach them to the form.

## Source of funds
1. Ask the customer where the money came from and how they came to have it.
2. Ask for supporting evidence. Examples: bank statements and withdrawal slips, business accounts, documents for the sale of a house or shares, injury award, inheritance, redundancy payment, gambling win.
3. Record the explanation and the documents received.
4. If the customer cannot explain or provide evidence, do not accept the cash. Tell a manager. Consider [[suspicious-transaction-reporting]].

## The cash transaction record
Complete one Cash Transaction Record for each cash transaction, or each linked group, of €10,000 or more. Keep it with the supporting documents.
1. Header: record reference, date opened, prepared by, business location, customer reference, log entry reference.
2. Customer details: type (individual, company, trust, other), name, trading name, date of birth or incorporation date, nationality, address, phone and email, occupation or business.
3. Identification: photo ID type and number, proof of address document, company registration, beneficial owner, authorised representative, whether customer, payer and collector match.
4. Cash transaction: date, receipt reference, buy or sell, metal and product, quantity, gross value in euro, cash amount, denominations, condition of notes, bank band details, single or linked, linked details, order channel (walk-in, phone, online), collection arrangements.
5. Source of funds and wealth: explanation, documents received, any third-party funding.
6. Screening: PEP, sanctions, adverse media, high-risk geography, customer risk rating, whether EDD is required.
7. Provenance (when metal is bought from a customer): how it was acquired, documents received, any concern about stolen goods or criminal property.
8. Reviewer assessment: summary, red flags, mitigating factors, rationale.
9. Outcome: whether MLRO review is required, MLRO or approver, outcome (proceed, proceed with conditions, hold or decline), whether a suspicious transaction report (STR) was considered and submitted, decision date.
10. Conditions and follow-up, and where the documents are stored.
11. Sign-off: prepared by, reviewed by, approved by, date.

Attach: copy of photo ID, copy of proof of address, copy of the invoice, copy of source-of-funds evidence.
